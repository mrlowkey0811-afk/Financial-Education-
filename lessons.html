Lessons

Six lessons. No jargon, no lectures.

Pick a topic and read it in five minutes or less.

Basics

Budgeting 101

Every dollar you get should have a job before you spend it — even if that job is just "sitting in savings." A budget is just a plan for where your money goes, made before you spend it instead of after. Try the budget calculator on the Tools page to build your own split.

Basics

Needs vs. wants

A need keeps you fed, clothed, safe, or able to get to school/work. Everything else is a want — even really good wants. Before buying something, ask: "would this still matter to me in a month?" If not, it's probably a want, and that's fine, just budget for it on purpose.

Saving

Saving for a goal

Vague goals like "I should save more" rarely work. Specific ones do: name the thing, the price, and the date you want it by. Divide the price by the weeks you have, and that's your weekly target. The savings tracker on the Tools page does this math for you.

Saving

How interest works

When you keep money in a savings account, the bank pays you a little extra for letting them hold it — that's interest. It seems tiny at first, but over years it compounds, meaning you start earning interest on your interest. Starting at 14 beats starting at 24, even with less money.

Earning

Your first paycheck

Your first paycheck is usually smaller than expected — taxes and deductions come out before it reaches you. Read every line once so it's not a surprise. A simple rule that works well: save at least 10% the moment it arrives, before you can spend it.

Mindset

Spending smart

Sales, limited-time offers, and "only 2 left" messages are designed to make you decide fast. Give yourself a 24-hour rule for anything over a set amount — if you still want it tomorrow, it's probably a real want, not just a reaction to pressure.
